### **20 Trivia Questions & Answers: Talk Like a Pirate Day**

**1\. Question:** International Talk Like a Pirate Day was originally chosen to fall on the birthday of which founder's ex-wife, simply because it was the only date he could reliably remember?

* **Answer:** **Mark Summers** (or "Slappy").  
* *Net Control Fact:* Summers and co-founder John Baur ("Ol' Chumbucket") created the holiday during a racquetball game in 1995\.

**2\. Question:** What nationally syndicated humor columnist made the holiday a worldwide phenomenon after dedicating a column to it in 2002?

* **Answer:** **Dave Barry.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* Barry loved the idea so much he endorsed it nationally, turning an obscure inside joke into an international event.

**3\. Question:** The Hollywood "pirate accent" (including the growled *"Arrr\!"*) was popularized by actor Robert Newton's portrayal of which fictional pirate in the 1950 film *Treasure Island*?

* **Answer:** **Long John Silver.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* Newton based the accent on the regional dialect of the West Country in England, where he grew up.

**4\. Question:** In traditional nautical and pirate slang, what is the term for a crew member or sailor's close friend or comrade?

* **Answer:** **Matey** (or **Shipmate**).

**5\. Question:** What was the traditional name given to the flag flown by European and American pirates depicting a skull and crossbones?

* **Answer:** **The Jolly Roger.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* The term likely derives from the French *joli rouge* ("pretty red"), referring to the blood-red flags originally flown to signify "no quarter given."

**6\. Question:** What was the real name of the English pirate known as "Blackbeard," who captained the *Queen Anne's Revenge*?

* **Answer:** **Edward Teach** (or Edward Thatch).

**7\. Question:** In pirate folklore, what was the ominous paper marker handed to a pirate as an ultimatum, notice of trial, or death sentence?

* **Answer:** **The Black Spot.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* This concept originated in Robert Louis Stevenson's 1883 novel *Treasure Island*.

**8\. Question:** What specific name is given to an officially licensed private captain authorized by a government during wartime to attack and capture enemy merchant vessels?

* **Answer:** **A Privateer.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* They were granted legal protection through a government commission called a **Letter of Marque**.

**9\. Question:** In pirate terminology, the phrase **"scuttle the ship"** means to do what to the vessel?

* **Answer:** **Deliberately sink it.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* This was typically done by holing the hull or opening valves so an enemy couldn't capture the vessel.

**10\. Question:** What Jamaican port city was famous during the 17th century as the "pirate capital of the Caribbean" before being largely destroyed by an earthquake in 1692?

* **Answer:** **Port Royal.**

**11\. Question:** In traditional nautical terminology, what was the beverage called **"grog"** originally made of?

* **Answer:** **Rum diluted with water** (often with lemon or lime juice added to prevent scurvy).  
* *Net Control Fact:* It was introduced in 1740 by British Vice Admiral Edward Vernon, nicknamed "Old Grog" because of the grogram coat he wore.

**12\. Question:** What gruesome naval punishment involved tying a rope to a crewman and dragging him underwater from one side of the vessel, underneath the hull, to the other?

* **Answer:** **Keelhauling.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* If the sailor didn't drown, the razor-sharp barnacles encrusting the ship's bottom usually inflicted severe wounds.

**13\. Question:** Which famous Welsh privateer and pirate sacked Panama City in 1671 and was later knighted by King Charles II, eventually becoming the Lieutenant Governor of Jamaica?

* **Answer:** **Sir Henry Morgan** (Captain Morgan).

**14\. Question:** In pirate folklore and maritime legend, what is the idiom used to describe the bottom of the sea as the final resting place of dead sailors?

* **Answer:** **Davy Jones' Locker.**

**15\. Question:** What is the maritime term for a sailor or crew officer responsible for the ship's rigging, anchors, sails, and deck operations, famously summoned via a shrill silver pipe or whistle?

* **Answer:** **Boatswain** (pronounced *Bosun*).

**16\. Question:** Which notorious female pirate sailed alongside "Calico Jack" Rackham and Anne Bonny during the Golden Age of Piracy, famously disguised in men's clothing?

* **Answer:** **Mary Read.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* Both Mary Read and Anne Bonny escaped immediate execution after capture by "pleading their bellies" (declaring they were pregnant).

**17\. Question:** What is the common nautical term for the raised deck at the very rear (aft) of a sailing ship, typically where the captain and officers maintained watch?

* **Answer:** **The Poop Deck.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* The term derives from the French word *la poupe*, meaning the stern.

**18\. Question:** In real 18th-century pirate crews, how was the captain chosen and held accountable?

* **Answer:** **By a democratic vote of the crew.**  
* *Net Control Fact:* Unlike traditional naval vessels with strict hierarchies, pirate ships operated under written "Articles" and could vote their captain out if they were unhappy with his leadership.

**19\. Question:** Operating off the coast of China in the early 19th century, history's most successful and powerful pirate leader commanded the Red Flag Fleet of several hundred ships. What was her name?

* **Answer:** **Ching Shih** (or Zheng Yi Sao / Madame Zheng).  
* *Net Control Fact:* She commanded upwards of 70,000 to 80,000 pirates and outfought the Qing dynasty, the Portuguese, and the British before negotiating an honorable retirement and keeping her loot.

**20\. Question:** What is the nautical phrase shouted by a lookout upon first sighting land from the crow's nest or masthead?

* **Answer:** **"Land ho\!"**
